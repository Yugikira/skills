# Definition of the nature of an experiment

> **Status**: Formalized into `kb-skill/extract/templates/paper_summary_experimental.md` (2026-05-15)

Abdel-Khalik and Ajinkya (1979):

> The researcher manipulates one or more variables with subjects who are assigned randomly to various groups. 
> These groups receive different combinations of the variables (termed `treatments`); in some cases a control 
> group may exist which receive no such treatments. The major advantage of experiments is the high internal validity, defined in terms of how well they can eliminate rival explanations.

# Guidelines for good experimental research

1. A clear statement of the problem, its importance and the contribution to knowledge that its solution will make.

2. A clear statement of the theory that underlies the process, in particular the theory that drives the behavior 
and the impact of context on theory.

3. A sound experimental design - since fatal flaws can be introduced by inappropriate or inadequate designs.

4. Recognition of the importance of external validity.

# Extraction Contents

Experimental research has different view point from archival and analytical research.

## Ground Theories

Unlike analytical and archival studies, where one is developing theory and the other only implicitly relies on theory and more relies on past findings, experimental research tends to clearly state the grounding theories.

Therefore the best practice is to first list the ground theories of the paper, and link to the paper first come up with the theory. And establish a stand-alone page for the theory.

For the theory page, related all drive theories/propositions from the original theory.

And use sections `Evidence support the theory` and `Evidence contradit to the theory` to relate theories to the source paper. 

## Claim Findings

The claim findings is not just the interpretation of the fundings. It is the statement of the problem and the interpretation of the experiment results.

The dependent variable and independent variables are stated in an abstract way similar to `concepts`. For example, `literacy`, `AI literacy` and `AI receptivity`.

## Ground Truth Findings

Similar to typical archival studies, the ground truth findings should be the most **objective** results.

The ground truth findings should be related to the claim findings toes to toes. And stated in below example form:

```Example
With the subjects from {brief introduction of the subjects' background}, participate in {study}. The manipulated variable {} is {how related to } the dependent variable.
```

## The Context

The question of context is a potentially great threat to the external validity of judgement in experiment tasks.

We may be unsure whether the observed findings are attributable to the variables subject to manipulation, or to the impact of such variables as `task complexity`, `location`, `time constraints`, `incentives` etc.  

Briefly summarize context related information, use numerical indicator or summaries about above variables.

## Experimental design

- Categorize the design into:

    - **Post-test only control group design**: Subjects are randomly assigned to treatments, that is to different levels of the independent (explanatory) variables.

    - **Pre-test/post-test control group design**: Subjects are measured to see how they react to successive treatments, but this time a before-and-after experimental design is used which allows the researcher to control for individual differences. The shortage of the design: the `learning effect` of the subjects.

    - **Factorial design - between subjects**: This involves the simultaneous variation of two or more treatments so that researcher can monitor their separate impacts on the dependent variable, and any potential interactive effect between the independent variables.

    - **Factorial design - within subjects**: As with the simpler design, researcher can also introduce a 'before -and-after' factorial design. Whereas in a 'between-subject' design subjects receive only one treatment, in a 'within-subjects' design each subject receives all the successive treatments. This constitutes what is often called a 'repeated measures' design.

- Briefly summarize the design study(studies).

- Briefly comment on below items within 3 sentences.

    - **Control group**: Is it a clean control group, that is to say all other conditions except the one examined is the same for control group and treatment group.

    - **Randomisation**: Is the distribution of the variable equally likely across all independent variables?

    - **Counterbalancing**: Especially for `factorial design - within subjects`, the order of receiving the treatments may affect the results. Does the researcher consider to mitigate this effect and how?
