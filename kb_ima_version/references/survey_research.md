# Definition of Survey Research

Survey methods are often criticised as being the 'poor man's experiment'. The main shortage is their 
inability to assign subjects randomly to treatments, and their consequent inability to rule out rival hypotheses.

Brown (1995) suggests that survey can be designed to minimise such threats, while optimising their externality 
validity benefits. He emphasises **the need for good theory in order to underpin the specification of causal relationships**.

In my opinion, well designed surveys are valuable even without any causal findings or conclusions, the truthfully 
reflection of the society itself is worthy and can be anecdotal evidence or supporting evidence of assumptions in other researches.

Surveys can be conducted via mail, telephone, e-mail, internet platform or face-to-face interview.

# Extraction

## Common information for all surveys

### Claim Findings

The interpreted results from the survey outcome

### Ground Truth Findings

The ground truth findings is the most objective results from the paper.

The ground truth findings should be related to the claim findings. And stated as below:
```
The survey Question {question} result shows that...

or

If the research conduct statistical analysis, describe as archival ground truth findings.
```

If the {question} is too long (over 3 questions), group them with a short summary, and indicate the question numbers.

### Concepts

The concepts of survey paper could be easier to extract. They often lies in the research question, the title, or the introduction.

### Variables

Different from other research methods, variables measuring concepts are usually scalar answers/yes or not answers to the questions. And one concept is measure by several dimensions (questions). Group them together, and summarize what questions related to the concepts.

For wiki pages, the variables (indeed questions in group) pages be explicitly marked as _survey.


## For non face-to-face interview surveys


### Survey Design

Briefly summarize the survey design including: 

- What sort of survey does the paper contemplate? 
- What sort of respondent does the paper target? (Similar to subjects in the experimental research)
- What response categories does the paper contemplate? - Asking for opinions, judgements or knowledge?
- What is the sequence of questions? From easiest (shortest) to hardest, or the other way around, or randomly.
- What is the layout of the survey instrument? Or what is the basic survey instrument？Self-reported performance, budgetary participation or tolerance of ambiguity. Use well-established instruments from previous research can ease the construct validity problem.
- If above answer is a new instrument, briefly summarize how the paper test the validity. **CREATE NEW wiki pages for new baseline instrument as methods** and explicitly marked as *_survey_instrument.md.
- What does the paper do to encourage respond from the subjects？ Does the paper address non-response issue and how?
- How does the paper address the reliability of the measures? For instance, `Test-Retest reliability`, `Split-half reliability coefficient`, `Cronbach's Aplpha Coefficient` and so forth.

## For face-to-face interview

Additional to the `Survey Design` in above section, should also address following questions:
- whether the interview is belong to `Structured Interview`, `Semi-Structured Interview` and `Unstructured interview`.
- How does the paper acknowledge the interviewees how and why they have been chosen to participate.
